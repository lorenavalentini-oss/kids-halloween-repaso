📖 The Story of Halloween

Every year, on October 31st, people in many countries celebrate Halloween. Today, children wear costumes, decorate houses and go trick-or-treating. But Halloween has a very long history!

More than 2,000 years ago, the Celts lived in parts of Ireland, Britain and other areas of Europe. They celebrated a festival called Samhain at the end of the harvest season. They believed that, at this time of year, the world of the living and the world of spirits were very close. People lit fires and sometimes wore disguises.

Later, Christian traditions influenced the celebration. The evening before All Saints' Day became known as All Hallows' Eve. Over time, the name changed to Halloween.

In the 19th century, Irish immigrants brought Halloween traditions to North America. The celebration changed over the years. Today, many families carve jack-o'-lanterns from pumpkins, dress up as witches, ghosts or superheroes, and enjoy parties. Children often knock on doors and say “Trick or treat!” to ask for sweets.

Halloween is not celebrated in exactly the same way everywhere. Some people love scary decorations, while others prefer funny costumes and games. For many children, it is a chance to be creative, spend time with friends and have fun!
